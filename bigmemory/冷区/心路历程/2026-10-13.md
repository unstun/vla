# From a custom 30 Hz push to Foxy Nav2, recovery-first repair and run-to-run variance

Archived on 2026-10-13 07:05 America/Los_Angeles. Stage: experiment + analysis.
Scope: Newton versions v47–v188, run 2026-10-02 to 2026-10-13. This record
gives the direction changes and why they happened. It is not a milestone
record, and it does not promote any label.

Unless noted, all paths are under
`.pipeline/experiments/2026-09-26_newton_stair_reproduction/`.

## 1. Shift from the custom 30 Hz local planner to an actual Foxy Nav2 stack (v47–v99, Oct 2–8)

What happened before the shift (v47–v85):

- Warm-start, cache and epoch work brought v58/v59 to 27.53/26.43 Hz of
  successful planning.
- Their maximum solve times were 659.5/1,236.7 ms, so sustained 30 Hz and tail
  elimination both failed.
- Asynchronous mapping (v79–v82) preserved the heartbeat but reached only
  11.1/11.0 Hz, with pauses of 23.8/31.3 s. Mapping kept breaching the
  unchanged 250 ms scan-age gate.
- v84 rejected latest-only admission because it lost obstacle evidence. v85
  remained a component prototype.
- v48 stayed the experimental reference throughout (`versions/README.md`,
  `versions/current.json`).

Why the direction changed:

- **Platform constraint, chosen by Dr Sun.** He selected the existing Jetson
  with Ubuntu 20.04 and ROS 2 Foxy because that robot cannot be upgraded to
  22.04. Source: `.pipeline/design/2026-10-08_nav2_foxy/bt_only_design_superseded.md`.
- **Requested architecture.** Dr Sun then asked for actual SCAN
  pluginization and Nav2-style mapping-backlog handling (v86/v87 `PLAN.md`;
  journal session 30). After that, he approved full Nav2 integration (v88–v99;
  `.pipeline/design/2026-10-08_nav2_foxy/design.md`). In this design, Nav2
  owns tasks and retries, PCT is a GlobalPlanner plugin, and SCAN is a
  Controller plugin. The native 3D checks and the measured-stop authority
  remain in charge.
- **Upstream norm.** The 2026-10-08 mapping survey found that published systems
  separate mapping, trajectory and control rates and bound admission. None
  maps every frame at control rate.
- **Delivery standard.** Dr Sun rejected delivery based on component tests
  alone and required the original simulation to work (journal session 32).
  That led to v100–v114 and the first Nav2 arrival in the original scene
  (v114).

The 30 Hz requirement was **not formally dropped**. Every later report still
lists sustained 30 Hz as unmet. The 2026-10-09 qualification survey changed
how this requirement is read: 30 Hz and 60 ms are the project's own acceptance
criteria, not an external rule that disqualifies a system. In practice,
completing whole missions under declared scenarios became the working gate.
Whether the 30 Hz acceptance should remain is an open question for Dr Sun, not
a decision recorded here.

## 2. MPPI-inspired incremental plan parked at step 3 (v122–v127, Oct 9–10)

On 2026-10-09, Dr Sun asked for five MPPI-inspired changes, applied one at a
time on gpu5070ti, with a keep-or-park decision for each. Source:
`.trellis/tasks/10-04-newton-v64-mac-physics/prd.md`.

| Step | Version | Change | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | v122 | Typed failure diagnostics | Kept as instrumentation only |
| 2 | v126 | One recovery chain per round | Parked: first physical trial failed under the predeclared early-stop rule |
| 3 | v127 | Separate validation interface | Parked |

Why step 3 was parked and steps 4–5 never started:

- Dr Sun asked to separate a regression caused by the change from instability
  in the original system.
- Six fresh runs per arm gave v122 0/6 arrivals (one fall) and v127 1/6.
- Captured inputs agreed exactly between the two: 28,720 curves, 11,691
  epochs and nine controller queries
  (`audits/2026-10-10_mppi_step3_repeats/REPORT.md`).
- The retained baseline itself was therefore unreliable in this scene. Further
  refactors could not be judged on an unstable base, and the shared
  rejection/recovery behaviour had to be diagnosed first.

## 3. Recovery-first repair (v128–v177, Oct 10–12)

The failures were stop/recovery chains, not slow average solves. Median solves
were already at millisecond scale. The chains were:

- safety revocation;
- stale input;
- a floor-support gap;
- a turn-sweep deadlock;
- a PCT singleton crash;
- an endpoint deadband.

What happened:

- **v128–v133.** Isolated fixes that did not establish reliability. v128 went
  2/6 against the original's 2/6. v131 went 1/6 with one fall and was not
  adopted.
- **v134–v139.** The v133 posture audit found the left front foot entering a
  floor gap about 7 cm wide. This led to a support-gap veto applied both
  locally and globally (v134/v135) and a wait for transient input (v137).
  v138 blocked the start and was rejected. v139 fed collision positions back
  to the optimizer and went 6/6, against 5/6 for v137. That result is
  validated for the declared corner scenario only
  (`audits/2026-10-10_v133_posture/RESULT.md`).
- **v144 onward.** Dr Sun clarified that the deliverable must start at the
  original spatial start and cover the whole route (v144 report). The
  corner-scenario success therefore did not satisfy the request.
- **Local reproduction.** Dr Sun authorized reproducing the run on the Mac
  (v149) before repeating it on Linux. The repairs then followed recorded
  failures one at a time:
  - v147: prefix proposal depth;
  - v151: interior curve samples;
  - v164: steering directions;
  - v167: short reverse;
  - v170/v172: progress renewal and the BT frame;
  - v174: PCT degenerate query;
  - v176/v177: finish radius changed from 15 cm to 2 cm.
- **Why one change per version, with a cohort.** Single arrivals kept proving
  nothing:
  - v147 made the first original-start full-route arrival on 2026-10-12
    (gpu5070ti, 76.883 m, 118.207 native s; v148 video). Unchanged repeats
    then went 0/3 closed before the reboot and 1/2 after, so full-route
    repeatability was not accepted.
  - On the Mac, the first passes were v160 attempt02 and v164, then v170 and
    v174. v174 (77.344 m) became the parent of the Linux ports.
  - v175, the Linux port of v174, went 2/5.

  Every candidate therefore needed a cohort of complete repetitions, with Mac
  and Linux cohorts never pooled. v177 (5/5 on Linux) was the first accepted
  repetition. See `bigmemory/冷区/里程碑/2026-10-13.md` for why v160 rather
  than v174 is recorded as the first Mac pass.

## 4. Stopping and the endpoint crawl: v185–v187 rejected (Oct 12–13)

- **The stop audit raised a locomotion question.** The v177 stop-cause audit
  found 0.957 m of drift under zero command. That made zero-command standing a
  locomotion question, separate from navigation.
- **Speed precondition corrected.** The v179 policy-only tests ran below the
  0.6 m/s cruise. Dr Sun caught this missing precondition, and v182 reran them
  at 0.6–1.0 m/s. The result made the diagnosis depend on walking speed. No
  standing controller was selected.
- **What the crawl is.** The RL speed audit found 9.15 s of correction after
  the spline expired, about 2.19 cm from the end of the curve, at 0.0175 m/s
  (`audits/2026-10-13_v177_rl_speed/REPORT.zh.md`).
- **Authorization.** Dr Sun authorized fixing the crawl with the RL model
  unchanged (task PRD, 2026-10-13).
- **Why the candidates were rejected.**

  | Version | Arrivals | Endpoint criterion |
  |---|---|---|
  | v185 | 3/5 | Failed |
  | v186 | 2/5 | Failed |
  | v187 | 3/5 | Passed, 5/5 |

  The unchanged v177 binary arrived 5/5 in the same period (attempts 06–10).
  Acceptance requires both full-route arrival and the endpoint criterion. A
  better endpoint metric alone was not enough to replace v177
  (`audits/2026-10-13_terminal_repair_review/REPORT.zh.md`).

## 5. The v188 unchanged-source control and what it implies

- **What v188 is.** v188 rebuilt the controller without changing any of the
  649 native source files. The controller's `.text`, `.dynstr` and
  `.data.rel.ro` sections are identical to v177's.
- **What it showed.** It still arrived only 3/5, so some route failures occur
  with no new logic at all. The cause of the variation is unknown
  (`versions/v188/RESULTS.md`).
- **Implication (computed by this writer).** With five trials per cohort, 5/5
  against 3/5 gives a two-sided Fisher exact p of about 0.44, and 5/5 against
  2/5 gives about 0.17. The v185–v187 rejections and the v177 5/5 are
  therefore both weak evidence about the effect of a candidate.
- **Proposal for the next step (not a decision; needs Dr Sun's authorization
  for remote runs):**
  1. Measure the run-to-run variance of the existing v177 binary with a larger
     same-host cohort.
  2. Identify the source of the nondeterminism, such as asynchronous
     scheduling or host load.
  3. Size candidate cohorts from that variance before another controller
     change.

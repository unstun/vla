## Preserve the user's selected timing and goal contract

Dr Sun explicitly selected local planning/controller 30 wall Hz, global
planning 1 wall Hz, goal XY tolerance 0.25 m and yaw tolerance 0.25 rad. With
no supplied final yaw, use the initial global path's last distinct horizontal
segment. The 0.20 m Z tolerance and measured stopped dwell are Lite3-specific
additions. Why: replacing the requested goal with a grid-snapped endpoint, or
changing yaw on each refresh, changes what arrival means. v36 preserves exact
goal identity and stop/preemption/emergency semantics; later performance work
retains this contract. These are current experiment choices, not universal
preferences for all future projects.

## Adopt the numerically qualified CPU graph, retain the failed CUDA evidence

v38 CUDA graph replay achieved about 1.58 physics-only RTF, but fixed-target
replay failed the declared 0.001 rad joint-error gate. The gate was not relaxed.
v40 CPU graph execution instead matched all 200 original CPU closed-loop
trace rows exactly and retained the per-step safety checks. Why: speed alone
would confound the planning diagnosis with a changed physical trajectory.
v41 integrated the CPU path and the qualified v39 cache; it did not promote a
component comparison into physical navigation acceptance. The CUDA candidate,
its failed replay and all frozen source versions remain available.

## Deliver a faithful wall-time video while reporting remaining failures

Dr Sun requested an ordinary playable video for visual inspection, with
physical simulation as close to wall-time 1:1 as possible. v42 and v45 paced
actual native progress without dropping physical steps. v45 separately
anchored navigation wall time after factory startup while native state and
time stayed continuous. v46 displays the latest causally available original
physical frame on the captured wall timeline; it adds no interpolation or
physical steps. Why: retiming native samples to look smooth would conceal the
pauses the user wants to inspect.

The delivered `output/2026-09-30_lite3_realtime/v45_actual_wall_57s.mp4`
contains 1711 frames at 30 FPS, 57.033333 s, within 11.048 ms of its captured
wall span. Full decoding and sampled frame inspection passed. This establishes
wall-time playback only. v45 physical navigation RTF was 0.9400067, local
planning 16.8619 Hz, raw controller 29.9648 Hz and observed global PCT 0.99998 Hz.
It stopped on `global_refresh_failed:pct_no_route`; no complete-route arrival
was accepted. Why retain this: the video is a useful review artifact even
though the requested physical 1:1/local 30 Hz contract remains unmet. v3's one
12 m empty-floor trial remains the last accepted navigation condition.

## Optimize the measured repeated work before promising 30 Hz

The v45 audit found 647 first-seed search failures among 878 completed rounds;
736 rounds ultimately succeeded using shorter reference prefixes. Single-
attempt rounds averaged 19.7 ms but still had 21 budget misses in 140 rounds;
two-attempt rounds averaged 56.5 ms. Why: this points to target/window selection
and initial-trajectory reuse as candidates worth testing before simply raising
a timer rate. The measured wall-log spans include printing/checks and are not
isolated A* CPU profiles. No A* 200 ms timeout or exact repeated same-mode
initializer was observed; retries cannot be blindly removed because targets
and inputs can differ.

The ranked proposals are: reuse a recently feasible local window; initialize
from the remaining valid trajectory; share a total solve/recovery budget; then
evaluate revision-aware raw classification caching and diagnostic output cost.
Each requires a separate version and current terrain, collision and PVA checks.
Why retain the distinction: v44's 11.8/12.8 ms recorded component solves did not
make the complete live callback 30 Hz. The proposals remain unimplemented and
v47 was not created. The global no-route failure is a separate open issue.

# Replanning rates, simulation clocks and CPU graph execution

Archived on 2026-10-01, America/Los_Angeles; source inspection and experiments
were performed on 2026-09-30. Stage: experiment + analysis. This record retains
the implications of inspected primary sources; it does not reproduce their
systems or establish full-building navigation acceptance.

## SCAN's timer rate is not its trajectory-generation rate

The [SCAN-Planner paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19555v1)
(`2606.19555v1`) describes onboard Jetson Orin NX operation and simulation on an
i9-14900K, but does not report a fixed local replanning frequency or achieved
30 Hz trajectory generation. At the inspected upstream commit
`348e8a590a50a5a6bbab8d8c6dcfd171f009be26`, the
[FSM](https://github.com/wuyi2121/SCAN-Planner/blob/348e8a590a50a5a6bbab8d8c6dcfd171f009be26/src/planner/plan_manage/src/scan_replan_fsm.cpp)
checks at 100 Hz and its safety callback at 20 Hz. The
[controller](https://github.com/wuyi2121/SCAN-Planner/blob/348e8a590a50a5a6bbab8d8c6dcfd171f009be26/src/planner/plan_manage/src/closed_loop_controller.cpp)
timer is configured at 100 Hz. New trajectories are triggered by displacement,
trajectory state and safety conditions; these timers do not establish 100 new
trajectories per second. The original planner also has a previous-trajectory
initialization branch. The local optimization proposal to restore that reuse
has not been implemented.

Why retain this: the selected local 30 Hz trajectory-generation requirement is
a new project requirement. It must not be attributed to the paper, or inferred
from a controller/FSM timer.

## Nav2 control scheduling and physical real time are separate contracts

At Nav2 commit `3c3db59d6969d8ecee8e68468693d006397f4a0c`,
[ControllerServer](https://github.com/ros-navigation/navigation2/blob/3c3db59d6969d8ecee8e68468693d006397f4a0c/nav2_controller/src/controller_server.cpp#L355)
uses `rclcpp::WallRate(controller_frequency_)` for computing and publishing
velocity commands and warns when that rate is missed. The inspected
[Jazzy controller documentation](https://docs.nav2.org/jazzy/configuration_and_development/configuration_guide/core_servers/controller_server/)
gives a 20 Hz default; the documented ordinary replanning behavior tree uses
global 1 Hz. These defaults/examples do not prescribe a universal robot
frequency, and Nav2's velocity update is not the same amount of work as
regenerating an entire SCAN trajectory.

The [ROS 2 clock design](https://design.ros2.org/articles/clock_and_time.html)
distinguishes ROS, system and steady clocks. `/clock` with `use_sim_time`
aligns message and trajectory time; it does not force physical simulation to
advance at wall speed. The inspected Gazebo, Isaac Sim, MuJoCo and Newton
mechanisms distinguish a target real-time factor, actual physical progress,
sensor publish rates and rendering FPS. A limiter can throttle a fast loop,
but cannot supply missing compute capacity. In the pinned Newton example
runner, `--realtime` raises process priority and `--render-fps` caps rendering;
neither automatically paces this project's custom headless episode.

Why retain this: assess controller Hz, new-trajectory Hz, global planning Hz,
physical RTF and video playback independently. A 30 FPS wall-time video can
faithfully show a slow or paused simulator. Complete primary URLs and source
pins are in the existing
[clock survey](../../../.pipeline/survey/2026-09-30_realtime_clock_upstream_survey.md)
and [continuous-planning survey](../../../.pipeline/survey/2026-09-30_continuous_local_planning_upstream_survey.md).

## CPU graph replay is supported upstream; numerical adoption is local evidence

The [Warp 1.17 runtime documentation](https://nvidia.github.io/warp/v1.17/user_guide/runtime.html)
describes experimental CPU graph capture: operations are deferred until graph
launch, and replay uses a C++ loop to reduce Python dispatch overhead. The
[pinned Newton AnyMal example](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/blob/c2ca70bec5998062b0b2d35869865cbe3a49feee/newton/examples/robot/example_robot_anymal_c_walk.py)
also supports CPU graph capture. This makes CPU graph execution a supported
mechanism to evaluate, not a guarantee of parity or full-system throughput.

Local v40 validation found exact equality for all 200 closed-loop trace rows
against the original CPU path, with continuous 4.1 s execution and every-step
capacity, applied-force, finiteness and counter checks. Its approximately
1.5845 physics-only RTF excludes sensing and transport. CUDA v38 was not
adopted: fixed-target replay exceeded the predeclared joint-error gate
(0.007906556 rad versus 0.001 rad), despite its speed gain. Why retain this:
upstream capability and local numerical qualification must both precede
adoption; graph speed alone cannot justify changing the physical execution
path or relaxing the gate.

Local evidence: `versions/v38`, `versions/v40` and
`audits/2026-09-30_gpu_device_compare` under the stair reproduction experiment.

binds exact runtime pins, comparisons and retained failure artifacts. No new
upstream source was acquired during this archive. No commit was created by
